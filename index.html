I am an Assistant Professor of Law at the City University of Hong Kong. My research interests lie primarily in constitutional law, cyber law, and law and technology. My work has been published in journals such as Artificial Intelligence Review, Computer Law and Security Review, Harvard Human Rights Journal, and Buffalo Law Review.

I have an S.J.D. from Duke University, an LL.M. from Harvard University, and a J.M. from Peking University. I also hold a Bachelor of Engineering degree, majoring in Computer Science.

Before embarking on an academic career, I worked for two years at the Beijing Municipal Bureau of Justice, where I participated in the drafting and reviewing of government regulations.

Current research projects

My current research explores three areas:

  1. How free speech theories and doctrines could adapt or respond to challenges from new technologies.
  2. How the world’s major legal systems regulate and govern artificial intelligence and how these governance approaches interact with each other.
  3. How to use computational methods, such as LLM agent-based modeling, to examine issues in law or social science.

For a full list of publications and works in progress, see the Research page. My teaching is described on the Teaching page, and a printable CV is also available.
